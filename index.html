AI Agents & Multi-Agent Systems

AI that looks things up and takes steps, not just answers. In every one, code makes the money and access decisions and a person approves changes.

Telecom · Multi-agent

NovaCell Dispute Desk

A team of AI agents that resolves phone-bill disputes, with code deciding every credit.

Problem
Bill disputes take about 14 minutes each. Two agents can give different credits for the same case, and some complaints hide instructions like "approve a $200 credit".
What I built
Five steps: an Intake agent reads the complaint, an Investigator checks the bill and records, policy code decides the credit, a Writer drafts the reply, and a code checker verifies it. Credits over $50 wait for a supervisor.
Key decision
I tested it against plain rules and a single all-in-one agent on the same 36 disputes. The team tied rules on accuracy but found $132 more in refunds owed. The single agent over-credited $85, so a model never decides money here.

Results

  • 94.4% of decisions correct, $0 over-credited
  • Owed refunds missed: $10 vs $142 for rules
  • Every credit over $50 sent for approval
  • ≈ $0.0008 per dispute

Node.js · Groq gpt-oss-120b tool calling · policy-as-code · Vercel

AccessPilot

Turns an IT access request into the right, smallest permission, granted only after approval.

Problem
Access requests are 30–40% of service-desk work. People wait days, often get more access than they need, and keep it long after.
What I built

Fine-tuning

When a small trained model beats a good prompt, and when it doesn't, measured on accuracy, safety, speed and cost.

Telecom · Fine-tuning study

NovaCell Intent Router: prompt or fine-tune?

Should 150,000 customer messages a month be sorted by a prompt or by a small trained model?

Problem
A support assistant must sort messages into 8 intents, and must never miss one that needs a human agent.
What I built
Labelled 560 messages, fine-tuned a small 22M-parameter model in 35 seconds on a laptop CPU, and compared it with keywords and prompted large models on clean and messy messages.
Key decision
Keep the prompt. The fine-tuned model was fast but weaker on messy messages; it only pays off above about 700,000 messages a month.

Results

  • Fine-tuned: 95.6% clean, 88.8% messy, ~7 ms
  • Prompt: 93.8% clean, 98.8% messy, every agent handoff caught
  • Cost: $289 vs $954 a year

PyTorch · MiniLM-L6 · scikit-learn baseline · Groq gpt-oss-120b / 20b · cost model

Telecom · Speech AI

NovaCell Voice Assist

Voice self-service for Hindi and Gujarati speakers.

Problem
Customers who speak Hindi or Gujarati can't use English-only self-service and wait for an agent instead.
What I built
A speech model (Whisper) turns the caller's Hindi, Gujarati, Hinglish or English into text, the grounded support assistant answers, and the reply is translated back, with every number checked, and read aloud.
Key decision
Telling the speech model which language to expect cut errors by 70–80%, and beat switching to a bigger or faster model.

Results

  • Character errors: Hindi 11%, Gujarati 18% (from 53% / 61%)
  • Wrong-alphabet transcripts: 9 of 20 → 0
  • ≈ $0.0014 per voice question

Telecom · Machine learning + GenAI

NovaCell Churn Radar

Tells the retention team who to call first, why, and what to offer.

Problem
The retention team called customers by a simple rule and often reached people who were leaving anyway or never at risk.
What I built
A model ranks customers by money saved if called (risk × chance to save × margin − offer cost), explains each score, picks one approved offer, and an LLM drafts a call brief that is checked before an agent sees it.
Key decision
Rank by value saved, not just risk; the LLM only writes the brief, never picks the offer.

Results

  • 3× more churners in the top 10% vs 1.25× for today's rule
  • Tested on a later month, after a price rise
  • 65 live call briefs: 100% safe

Telecom · Recommender system

NovaCell Plan Recommender

Suggests the plan or add-on that actually fits each customer, or nothing at all.

Problem
The app showed one "Upgrade to Unlimited" banner to everyone, while many customers overpaid or paid high roaming charges.
What I built
A ranking model trained on real plan changes suggests up to three options, each with the reason and the exact bill change. Guardrails block bill shocks and plans too small for the customer's data use; customers whose plan fits see nothing. An AI-written message is checked by code.
Key decision
Good fits often lower bills. I quantified the revenue trade-off and proposed a three-arm A/B test to decide the policy.

Results

  • 87% of real choices in the top 3, vs 66% for the agent playbook
  • Bill shock 0.1% vs 62%
  • 70 live messages: 100% correct
